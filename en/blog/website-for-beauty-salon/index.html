What website does a beauty salon need?

A strong salon website does more than display attractive photographs. It helps a client choose a service, understand the price, trust a specialist and book a suitable time.

1 August 20269 min readSHTR Studio
Primary goal

Turn scattered information into one clear route: service → confidence → booking.

A salon client may arrive from an ad, Instagram, a recommendation or Google. Within a few minutes, they want to know whether the service is available, what it costs, who will perform it, where the salon is and how to reserve a time. If every answer is hidden in different highlights, many potential clients simply move on.

What the website should do

A professional salon website performs three roles. It supports the brand image through colour, style, atmosphere and service quality. It helps visitors choose without a long conversation. Finally, it turns that decision into a booking through a clear button, messenger or calendar.

A beautiful page is only the beginning. Results appear when design, information and booking work together as one experience.

The ideal beauty salon website structure

1. First screen

Show the salon name or logo, a specific description, location, strong real photography and a clear “Book now” action. An emotional phrase can build atmosphere, but it should sit beside concrete services such as nails, brows, lashes, hair or full-service beauty.

2. Services and current pricing

Group services into understandable categories. Each should include a short explanation, typical duration and a clear price or “from” value. If the final price depends on hair length, design complexity or materials, explain this before booking.

3. Specialists

A photograph, name, specialty, experience and selected work help a visitor choose a person, not only a procedure. This is essential when the booking system asks them to select a specialist.

4. Portfolio

Organise work by service instead of publishing one random gallery. Images should be clear, authentic and visually consistent. Before-and-after examples work particularly well when accompanied by a short, factual description.

5. Reviews and proof

Real reviews, salon photography, information about hygiene, products and specialist training remove common doubts. Focus on evidence that influences the decision rather than overwhelming the visitor with every certificate.

6. Booking and contact details

The booking action should remain easy to find. Add phone, Instagram, messenger, address, map, opening hours and simple directions to the entrance. A salon with one location does not need a complicated branch selector.

How to organise online booking

There are two common starting points. A button can open Instagram Direct, Telegram or a phone call. This is simple, but a person still has to check availability manually. Alternatively, connect a platform such as EasyWeek, SimplyBook.me, Fresha or another service.

The salon owner creates services, specialists, working hours, breaks and cancellation rules. Clients see available times and book independently. The account, password and paid subscription should belong to the salon; the website simply provides the polished customer route into that system.

OptionAdvantageConsideration
Booking by messageFast and simple to launchAvailability must be checked manually
Booking platformCalendar, specialists, reminders and reportingRequires initial setup and may have a subscription
Custom booking systemComplete control over the logicHighest build cost, server and ongoing support

Materials to prepare

  • A high-quality logo or salon wordmark.
  • A complete service list with pricing and duration.
  • Photographs of the salon, team and strongest work by category.
  • Specialist names, roles and concise experience summaries.
  • Address, opening hours, contact details, social links and booking policies.
  • Client reviews that can be published.

If some materials are missing, begin with the structure and a clear production list. That is much better than filling an important page with unrelated stock photography.

Using the website for advertising

An ad for a specific service should lead to the matching section or page, not just a generic homepage. A lash-lamination campaign can immediately show the result, benefits, duration, price, FAQs and booking action. The client does not need to search.

Dedicated service pages also help search engines understand what the salon offers and where it operates. For local visibility, keep the business name, address, phone and opening hours accurate and consistent across the website and relevant platforms.

Common mistakes

  • A vague first screen that does not state the services or location.
  • A price list available only as a tiny image that is difficult to read on mobile.
  • Outdated prices or services without clear conditions.
  • Generic stock photos instead of real work and the actual salon.
  • A booking button that fails or requires too many steps.
  • Important information available only on Instagram.

Choosing the right format

A focused one-page website is often enough for a small studio: services, pricing, portfolio, reviews, contact details and booking. A salon with several specialists benefits from dedicated service and team pages. Multiple locations require a clear branch structure.
