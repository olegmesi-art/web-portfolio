How much does a business website cost in 2026?

There is no universal price. The budget depends on the business goal, number of unique pages, design, content, features and integrations. Here is what you are actually paying for.

1 August 20269 min readSHTR Studio
Short answer

Define the outcome and essential features first. Then the scope, timeline and price can be fixed with confidence.

Asking “How much does a website cost?” is similar to asking how much a car costs. One company needs a focused page for an ad campaign; another needs a multilingual catalogue with payments and customer accounts. Both are called websites, yet the work behind them is completely different.

Start with the right format

FormatBest forUsually includes
Landing pageOne service, campaign or new productOne focused page, benefits, proof, FAQ and enquiry form

The cheapest option is not always the smallest site. The most efficient option contains everything required for the client's decision and nothing the business does not need yet.

Six factors that shape the price

1. Strategy and structure

The team needs to understand the client, their problem, objections and desired next step. A clear structure shortens the route to an enquiry and prevents the project from becoming a random collection of sections.

2. Content

Copy, photography, video, pricing, specifications and reviews all affect scope. Ready, accurate materials speed up the project. Research, editing or production adds another layer of work.

3. Design

An established visual identity requires less exploration than a brand direction built from scratch. Unique page layouts, bespoke graphics, animation and detailed mobile behaviour all influence the effort.

4. Development

A static presentation is simpler than filters, accounts, databases or complex calculations. Every feature has to be implemented, tested and adapted for different screen sizes.

5. Integrations

Booking, payments, CRM, delivery, analytics and notifications may rely on third-party systems. Integrating a proven platform is often more efficient than building the same capability from zero.

6. Launch and support

Forms, links, performance, responsive layouts and basic SEO need checking before publication. Content updates, backups and technical support may continue after launch.

A reliable estimate is not a random number. It is a defined list of pages, features and materials with an agreed outcome, timeline and responsibility.

Possible ongoing costs

  • Domain. A custom brand address is usually renewed annually.
  • Hosting or server. A simple static website may use free hosting; dynamic systems need suitable infrastructure.
  • External services. Booking, CRM, email tools or paid modules may have separate subscriptions.
  • Growth. SEO, advertising and ongoing content are not always part of the initial build.

These costs should be clear before work begins. The domain, booking account and paid subscriptions should normally belong to the business owner, so the company keeps full control.

How to protect the budget without reducing quality

  • Launch the first useful version instead of adding every future idea at once.
  • Prepare current services, prices, photographs and contact information early.
  • Choose one primary action: enquiry, call, message or booking.
  • Use an established service for a complex function when it meets the need.
  • Consolidate feedback into clear review rounds rather than constantly changing direction.

What we need for an accurate estimate

Five short answers are usually enough to begin: what the business offers, who the customer is, which services or products need presenting, what the main website action should be and which features are essential. We then build the structure and price the defined scope.

At SHTR Studio, we separate the launch essentials from features that can be added later. This helps a business go live faster and avoid unnecessary first-stage costs.

Conclusion

The website budget is shaped by the business problem, not the number of decorative sections. When comparing proposals, check the structure, mobile experience, content, integrations, SEO foundations and post-launch terms — not only the final number.
